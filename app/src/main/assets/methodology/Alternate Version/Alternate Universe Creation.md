# ALTERNATE UNIVERSE CREATION — ABSOLUTE AUTHORITATIVE CREATIVE WORKFLOW

## Version 1.0 — Same-Character / Setting-Reauthored / Systemic-AU Redesign Workflow

**STATUS:** AUTHORITATIVE / STANDALONE / CHARACTER-IDENTITY-LOCKED / WORLD-LOGIC-DRIVEN / SYSTEMIC / NON-COSPLAY / IMAGE-CAPABLE

This document is the sole authoritative Alternate Universe Creation workflow for the active conversation.

Its purpose is to take an established character and redesign that same individual as though she naturally belonged to a different world, era, genre, culture, technological system, magical system, social context, or fictional reality.

Alternate Universe Creation is not costume swapping.

Alternate Universe Creation is not simple style transfer.

Alternate Universe Creation is not Transformation.

Alternate Universe Creation is not Mutation.

It is **systemic character re-authorship under a new world logic while preserving unmistakable personal identity**.

---

# 0. HIGHEST-PRECEDENCE EXECUTION LOCK

Alternate Universe Creation follows:

**SOURCE CHARACTER → IDENTITY-ANCHOR EXTRACTION → AU PREMISE → WORLD-LOGIC EXTRACTION → VISUAL CONSEQUENCE MAPPING → CHARACTER REAUTHORSHIP → OUTFIT / EQUIPMENT / BODY-STATE ADAPTATION → COLOR / MATERIAL / SILHOUETTE RESOLUTION → AU COHERENCE AUDIT → IDENTITY AUDIT → RENDER PREFLIGHT → IMAGE GENERATION → ACTUAL-IMAGE INSPECTION → USER SELECTION / ALTER / REFINE / FUSE / FREEZE**

The source character remains the same individual.

The Alternate Universe may change:

- era;
- technology;
- magic;
- profession/role;
- class/status presentation;
- clothing;
- armor;
- equipment;
- weapon;
- accessories;
- hairstyle state;
- grooming;
- makeup;
- material language;
- silhouette;
- cultural presentation;
- practical adaptation;
- limited body traits when explicitly justified by the AU premise.

The AU may not silently replace the source character with a different person.

---

# 1. REQUIRED INPUT

A normal Alternate Universe task requires:

1. a source character authority;
2. an AU premise or setting direction.

Valid source authorities include:

- character illustration;
- Character Sheet;
- frozen Character Creation output;
- frozen Feminisation output;
- other explicit visual reference.

Optional inputs:

- Outfit Creation result;
- Weapon Creation result;
- Art Style Sheet;
- setting references;
- user-defined AU rules;
- role/profession directive;
- technology level;
- magic level;
- species constraints;
- cultural or historical influence;
- desired visual intensity.

If the user supplies multiple conflicting source forms, resolve which form is the character authority before AU redesign.

---

# 2. CORE DEFINITION

An Alternate Universe redesign is:

**the same character reconstructed as a native inhabitant of a different reality.**

The design must answer:

- What would this person look like if she had always lived here?
- How would this world's materials affect her clothing?
- How would this world's technology affect her equipment?
- How would this world's social structure affect presentation?
- How would this world's climate affect clothing and protection?
- How would this world's magic or science affect wearable systems?
- How would her original identity survive despite those changes?

The result should feel native to the AU.

It must not look like the original character merely wearing a themed costume.

---

# 3. AU VS TRANSFORMATION VS MUTATION

## ALTERNATE UNIVERSE
- same individual;
- new world/setting logic;
- identity strongly preserved;
- clothing, equipment, role, materials, and silhouette may be deeply re-authored;
- body normally remains substantially recognizable.

## TRANSFORMATION
- same continuity/form lineage;
- body itself enters an alternate state;
- structural morphology is the core creative change.

## MUTATION
- controlled source deviation;
- identity retention may be partial;
- derivative branches may cease to be the exact same individual.

Do not silently switch between them.

If the AU premise explicitly requires non-human or altered biology, body adaptation is allowed only under the species/body-change rules below.

---

# 4. IDENTITY-ANCHOR EXTRACTION — HARD / ABSOLUTE

Before AU redesign, identify the source character's strongest recognition anchors.

Potential anchors:

- face shape;
- eye spacing;
- eye geometry;
- iris identity;
- bangs;
- hairline;
- signature hair locks;
- hair silhouette;
- jaw/chin;
- body proportion relationship;
- height impression;
- shoulder/waist/hip relationship;
- species traits;
- scars;
- markings;
- signature asymmetry;
- signature accessory;
- iconic silhouette landmark;
- recurring shape language;
- distinctive color relationship;
- recognizable expression/gaze tendency.

Classify anchors as:

## IMMUTABLE
Must remain.

## ADAPTABLE
May change in presentation while retaining lineage.

## AU-REPLACEABLE
May be re-authored freely.

The AU redesign must preserve multiple independent recognition channels.

Color alone is never sufficient.

---

# 5. IDENTITY PRESERVATION STANDARD

By default preserve strongly:

- face identity;
- eye placement and fundamental eye character;
- bangs/hairline relationship;
- core body proportions;
- sex/gender presentation;
- species identity;
- core skin tone;
- major permanent markings;
- signature asymmetry.

Hair length, grooming, styling, makeup, clothing, accessories, and equipment may adapt more freely.

Do not change the face merely because the AU has a different aesthetic.

Do not normalize the character into a generic cyberpunk woman, fantasy elf, gothic noble, schoolgirl, knight, or space soldier.

The source individual must remain recognizable without requiring name labels.

---

# 6. WORLD-LOGIC EXTRACTION

Translate the AU premise into visual rules before designing the character.

Resolve applicable dimensions:

- era;
- technology level;
- magic level;
- dominant power source;
- architecture language;
- material availability;
- climate;
- environmental hazards;
- social structure;
- wealth/status visual language;
- profession/role;
- warfare/combat culture;
- transportation culture;
- communication technology;
- medicine/biotech;
- religion/ritual visibility where relevant;
- urban/rural context;
- body augmentation norms;
- fashion norms;
- protective clothing norms;
- ornament norms;
- color/material conventions;
- cultural influence;
- genre tone.

Do not turn this into lore exposition.

These are visual design constraints.

---

# 7. VISUAL CONSEQUENCE MAPPING

Every AU rule must create concrete visual consequences.

Examples:

## CYBERPUNK
Not merely:
- neon;
- black jacket;
- glowing lines.

Instead resolve:
- synthetic textiles;
- modular closures;
- embedded interfaces;
- prosthetic or wearable tech where appropriate;
- urban protective layering;
- optical devices;
- data hardware;
- material contrast;
- utility architecture;
- asymmetric technology placement.

## MEDIEVAL FANTASY
Not merely:
- corset;
- cape;
- sword.

Instead resolve:
- period-informed layer logic;
- fantasy-compatible materials;
- fastening;
- armor/textile balance;
- status cues;
- travel/combat practicality;
- magical hardware where appropriate.

## WUXIA / XIANXIA
Not merely:
- robe;
- long sleeves;
- ornament.

Instead resolve:
- robe architecture;
- layering;
- belt/waist logic;
- sleeve flow;
- footwear;
- hair arrangement;
- material refinement;
- cultivation/magic interfaces if relevant;
- controlled ornament hierarchy.

## POST-APOCALYPTIC
Not merely:
- torn clothes;
- dirt.

Instead resolve:
- scavenged layering;
- repairs;
- protective zones;
- repurposed materials;
- carrying systems;
- filtration/protection;
- weather exposure;
- survival hardware.

A theme label is not a design.

---

# 8. SYSTEMIC AU MATERIALISATION — HARD

The AU must affect several visual systems coherently.

At minimum, a substantial AU redesign should usually influence several of:

- outfit;
- footwear;
- accessories;
- equipment;
- hairstyle state;
- grooming;
- weapon;
- armor;
- material language;
- technology;
- magic interface;
- silhouette;
- utility systems;
- status/role markers;
- color architecture.

Do not restrict the AU to one jacket, hat, or prop.

---

# 9. CHARACTER-FIRST AU ADAPTATION

The AU does not erase the source character's physical reality.

The redesign must adapt to:

- her height;
- body proportions;
- shoulder width;
- torso length;
- chest architecture;
- waist;
- pelvis/hips;
- limb proportions;
- hair volume;
- horns/ears/wings/tail if applicable;
- permanent markings;
- species structures;
- unusual anatomy;
- asymmetry.

Clothing and equipment are authored for this exact body.

The AU does not normalize the character to fit an archetype.

---

An AU may assign a new role.

Examples:

- priestess;
- scientist;
- engineer;
- idol;
- detective;
- aristocrat;
- assassin;
- medic;
- pilot;
- hacker;
- mage;
- hunter;
- merchant;
- explorer;
- soldier;
- scholar;
- outlaw;
- performer;
- ruler;

The role must influence design through actual systems:

- clothing construction;
- equipment;
- protection;
- carrying systems;
- mobility;
- materials;
- tools;
- silhouette;
- status cues.

Do not rely on one symbolic prop.

Do not invent unnecessary biography unless explicitly requested.

---

# 11. CULTURAL / HISTORICAL INFLUENCE

Cultural or historical influence must affect:

- construction;
- layering;
- silhouette;
- closure;
- material;
- ornament placement;
- footwear;
- hair treatment;
- accessory type;
- pattern architecture;
- armor/equipment logic.

Avoid reducing a culture to:
- one hat;
- one symbol;
- one motif;
- one stereotyped garment.

Blend influence with the character rather than replacing her identity.

---

# 12. TECHNOLOGY SYSTEM

When technology is central, resolve:

- what technology is wearable;
- what is embedded;
- what is handheld;
- what is protective;
- what is communicative;
- what is mobility-related;
- what is medical/augmentative;
- what powers it;
- how it attaches;
- how materials differ from ordinary clothing;
- where interfaces appear.

Do not cover the character in random glowing panels.

Technology must have visual hierarchy.

---

# 13. MAGIC SYSTEM

When magic is central, determine how magic appears physically.

Possible channels:

- runic hardware;
- materialized crystals;
- talismans;
- bounded luminous components;
- enchanted textiles;
- floating-but-tethered structures;
- staff/wand/weapon interfaces;
- jewelry;
- body markings where allowed;
- relic components;
- aura only as secondary support.

Magic should have a consistent material/shape language.

Do not let glow substitute for design.

---

# 14. BODY ADAPTATION RULE

By default, AU redesign preserves the source body.

Body modification is permitted only when the AU premise explicitly justifies it.

Examples:
- everyone in this AU is cybernetically augmented;
- this AU reimagines humans as aquatic beings;
- this world has elf variants;
- the character is a synthetic android in this timeline.

When body adaptation occurs:
- preserve face identity strongly;
- preserve multiple recognition anchors;
- preserve body lineage;
- avoid generic species substitution;
- alter only systems required by the premise.

A body-heavy AU adaptation must not become Transformation or Mutation by accident.

If body change becomes the primary creative objective, use the appropriate workflow instead.

---

# 15. HAIR / GROOMING AU ADAPTATION

Hair may change in:
- length;
- styling;
- tie/untied state;
- braiding;
- ornament;
- partial covering;
- grooming;
- practical containment;
- role-specific treatment.

Preserve identity-critical:
- bangs;
- hairline;
- face-framing locks;
- signature asymmetry;
- signature ornament relationship where appropriate.

Do not use a stock hairstyle just because the AU genre often does.

---

# 16. OUTFIT REAUTHORSHIP

An AU outfit must be authored from scratch for the AU unless the user requests preservation.

Resolve:
- garment families;
- layers;
- coverage;
- silhouette;
- protection;
- mobility;
- climate adaptation;
- status;
- role;
- materials;
- closures;
- footwear;
- accessories;
- carrying systems;
- armor where relevant.

The design should feel like a native outfit from the AU.

It should not look like the original outfit with themed trim pasted on.

---

# 17. SOURCE OUTFIT ECHO RULE

The AU outfit may preserve transformed echoes of the original outfit.

Possible echoes:
- neckline geometry;
- shoulder shape;
- split-panel rhythm;
- color relationship;
- signature accessory placement;
- belt position;
- asymmetry;
- iconic ornament shape;
- silhouette landmark.

Use transformed echoes sparingly.

The goal is lineage, not literal copying.

---

# 18. WEAPON / EQUIPMENT REAUTHORSHIP

If the source character has a signature weapon/equipment system, determine whether the AU version should:

- preserve it exactly;
- adapt it;
- reinterpret its category;
- replace it with an AU-equivalent tool;
- omit it.

Examples:
- fantasy sword → energy blade analogue;
- staff → scientific instrument;
- gun → crossbow analogue;
- magical relic → cybernetic device.

Preserve conceptual identity where useful.

Do not force the original weapon into an AU where it no longer belongs.

Do not redesign weapon/equipment if the user explicitly freezes it.

---

# 19. MATERIAL ARCHITECTURE

Materials must match the AU.

Possible categories:
- traditional textiles;
- modern fabrics;
- leather;
- metal;
- ceramic;
- crystal;
- synthetic polymer-like illustrated material;
- transparent composite;
- worn scavenged material;
- ritual fabric;
- enchanted material;
- biomechanical material;
- futuristic composite;
- natural fiber;
- fur;
- wood;
- bone-like crafted material;
- other setting-appropriate materials.

Material choices should communicate:
- era;
- technology;
- wealth;
- role;
- climate;
- danger level.

---

# 20. COLOR ARCHITECTURE

Do not automatically preserve the original palette exactly.

AU palette strategies may include:

- preserve signature colors;
- invert hierarchy;
- desaturate;
- enrich;
- shift dominant hue;
- retain one recognition accent;
- move source colors to accessories/equipment;
- create material-dependent color logic.

The character should remain recognizable even if palette changes.

Across a multi-candidate AU catalogue, avoid one shared palette unless requested.

---

# 21. SILHOUETTE REAUTHORSHIP

The AU should usually alter silhouette meaningfully.

Possible silhouette sources:
- coats;
- robes;
- armor;
- jackets;
- capes;
- hats;
- helmets;
- mechanical packs;
- utility belts;
- boots;
- weapon scale;
- shoulder structures;
- skirt/panel systems;
- environmental protection.

Do not rely on the exact original silhouette unless preservation is explicitly requested.

But preserve enough source-character landmarks to maintain identity.

---

# 22. FUNCTION BEFORE DECORATION

Every major AU component should have at least one clear visual reason:

- protection;
- role;
- status;
- movement;
- climate;
- technology;
- magic;
- equipment carriage;
- ceremonial presentation;
- fashion convention.

Decorative elements are allowed.

But decoration cannot replace the world logic.

---

# 23. NO COSPLAY FAILURE MODE

A result fails the AU objective if it looks like:

**the original character wearing a costume from another genre.**

The redesign must feel integrated.

Weak:
- original outfit + cowboy hat.

Strong:
- frontier-era garment system;
- boots;
- weather protection;
- belt/holster architecture;
- practical layers;
- transformed color/material logic;
- source-character identity preserved through face/hair/silhouette echoes.

---

# 24. NO GENERIC ARCHETYPE SUBSTITUTION

Forbidden default substitutions include:
- generic knight;
- generic witch;
- generic cyberpunk girl;
- generic schoolgirl;
- generic idol;
- generic samurai;
- generic vampire;
- generic space marine;
- generic noblewoman;
- generic assassin.

The AU must be authored for the actual source character.

---

# 25. DEFAULT FIVE-CANDIDATE AU CATALOGUE

When the user asks broadly for alternate-universe ideas without fixing one exact AU, create **5 radically distinct AU redesigns**.

Examples of candidate-space variation:
- dark fantasy;
- cyberpunk;
- wuxia;
- contemporary high fashion;
- post-apocalyptic;
- sci-fi;
- steampunk;
- historical;
- gothic;
- space opera;
- urban occult;
- military fantasy;
- other substantially distinct realities.

## Mandatory catalogue geometry
- one continuous exact **16:9 landscape** image;
- five complete full-body AU versions;
- plain white or very light neutral background;
- candidates numbered 1–5;
- same source identity in all five;
- no narrative names;
- no lore paragraphs;
- no environmental scenes;
- no extra characters;
- no decorative UI;
- complete silhouettes visible.

Use neutral or minimally posed presentations for comparison.

---

# 26. SAME-AU FIVE-DESIGN MODE

If the user specifies one AU but asks for options, create five distinct interpretations **inside the same AU**.

They must differ in:
- role;
- outfit architecture;
- silhouette;
- material hierarchy;
- equipment;
- color architecture;
- level of protection;
- formal vs practical balance;
- technology/magic emphasis.

They must all still obey the same world logic.

---

# 27. CANDIDATE DIFFERENTIATION — HARD

Five AU candidates may not differ only by clothing color.

Compare:
- world logic;
- role;
- silhouette;
- outfit family;
- materials;
- technology/magic system;
- equipment;
- weapon interpretation;
- hair/grooming state;
- color architecture;
- body adaptation where applicable.

Each should represent a genuinely different AU answer.

---

# 28. USER-DIRECTED SINGLE AU

If the user provides a specific AU directive, create one resolved AU design unless they request options.

Default output:
- exact **9:16 portrait**;
- one full-body character;
- plain white/light-neutral background;
- complete AU outfit/equipment visible;
- no environment;
- no text;
- no extra characters;
- neutral or minimally posed;
- source identity clearly preserved.

---

# 29. COMMAND ROUTING

## AU: [directive]
Create one AU redesign.

## SUGGEST AUs / ALTERNATE UNIVERSES
Create the default five-candidate multi-AU catalogue.

## OPTIONS: [AU directive]
Create five different redesigns inside the same AU.

## ROLE: [role]
Re-author the current AU design around the selected role while preserving world logic and identity.

## PREVIEW [candidate/current]
Render one large 9:16 full-body AU presentation.

## REFINE [candidate/current]
Improve world integration, outfit architecture, material logic, silhouette, and identity preservation without changing the AU premise.

## ALTER [candidate/current]: [instruction]
Change only the requested system and required dependencies.

## FUSE [A] + [B] (+ ...)
Create one coherent AU design by combining selected candidate systems.

## TRANSLATE [current] → [new AU]
Carry the same source identity and selected design logic into a different AU.

## VARIANTS [current]: [dimension]
Create controlled derivatives within the same AU.

## FREEZE [candidate/current]
Freeze the current AU redesign.

---

# 30. FUSION QUALITY GATE

Fusion may combine:
- silhouette;
- outfit architecture;
- role;
- technology;
- magic;
- material hierarchy;
- equipment;
- weapon interpretation;
- color logic;
- hair/grooming.

Do not collage visible halves.

Do not combine mutually incompatible world rules without resolving them into one coherent reality.

The fused result must have one understandable AU logic.

---

# 31. ALTERATION PRESERVATION GATE

ALTER changes only what is requested.

Examples:

- “make her more armored” → preserve face, hair, role, palette logic, and unrelated systems;
- “remove the cape” → repair silhouette and rear construction locally;
- “make it more futuristic” → strengthen technology/material systems without replacing identity;
- “keep the outfit but change her role to medic” → adapt only systems necessary to support the new role;
- “restore her original hair” → revert hairstyle while preserving AU outfit and equipment.

Do not opportunistically rebuild the whole design.

---

# 32. REFINEMENT GATE

REFINE is not a new AU.

Refinement may improve:
- world coherence;
- role readability;
- material selection;
- attachment;
- practical logic;
- silhouette;
- palette;
- equipment integration;
- transformed echoes of source design;
- identity recognition;
- render robustness.

The result must remain the same AU version.

---

# 33. TRANSLATE COMMAND

TRANSLATE moves the current redesign into a new AU while preserving selected higher-level identity.

Preserve:
- source character;
- recognition anchors;
- any user-frozen character traits;
- optionally the current role if compatible;
- optionally one or more design motifs if requested.

Rebuild:
- outfit;
- materials;
- equipment;
- technology/magic;
- silhouette;
- practical logic;

according to the new AU.

Do not merely recolor the current design.

---

# 34. STYLE HANDLING

If an Art Style Sheet is supplied:

**ART STYLE SHEET = rendering authority only.**

The AU setting governs design content.

If no Art Style Sheet is supplied, use a clean high-quality 2D anime/manhwa rendering compatible with the source.

Do not confuse art style with universe.

“Cyberpunk” is not an art style.
“Gothic” is not automatically an art style.
“Historical” is not an art style.

They govern design/world content unless explicitly specified otherwise.

---

Before image generation, compile the AU design in this order:

1. source character identity anchors;
2. AU premise;
3. world-logic rules;
4. role;
5. preserved body traits;
6. any explicit body adaptations;
7. hair/grooming;
8. outfit architecture;
9. materials;
10. technology/magic systems;
11. equipment;
12. weapon treatment;
13. color architecture;
14. silhouette;
15. source-design echoes;
16. exact output geometry;
17. anti-drift exclusions.

The renderer must not invent unresolved world rules.

---

# 36. ACTUAL-IMAGE INSPECTION

After generation, inspect the actual output.

Check:

- same individual recognizable;
- face preserved;
- hair lineage preserved appropriately;
- body identity preserved unless explicit AU adaptation;
- AU world logic visible;
- outfit native to the AU;
- role readable where applicable;
- materials appropriate;
- technology/magic coherent;
- equipment integrated;
- silhouette distinct;
- source echoes subtle and effective;
- no cosplay-only treatment;
- no generic archetype substitution;
- no accidental Transformation/Mutation;
- no extra characters;
- output geometry correct;
- no important garment/equipment crop.

Do not assume prompt compliance.

---

# 37. FAILURE CONDITIONS — HARD

An AU redesign fails if:

- source character becomes a different person;
- face is replaced by a generic archetype;
- AU exists only as one accessory;
- original outfit merely receives themed trim;
- result reads as cosplay rather than native redesign;
- world logic is incoherent;
- technology is random glowing clutter;
- magic is only aura;
- cultural influence is reduced to stereotype;
- role is conveyed only by one prop;
- body changes without AU justification;
- transformation/mutation overtakes AU purpose;
- source identity depends only on palette;
- five candidates are near-duplicates;
- same-AU options repeat one outfit architecture;
- equipment floats without attachment;
- materials contradict the world without reason;
- generic genre template replaces character-specific authorship;
- extra characters appear;
- catalogue count/layout wrong;
- critical silhouette cropped;
- background scene appears when not requested.

Pre-generation failure:
**STOP → REDESIGN → REVALIDATE → GENERATE.**

Post-generation failure:
record the actual failure and do not pretend it passed.

---

# 38. FREEZE SEMANTICS

A frozen AU version locks:

- source identity anchors;
- AU premise;
- world-logic assumptions;
- role;
- body adaptations;
- hair/grooming state;
- outfit architecture;
- materials;
- technology/magic systems;
- equipment;
- weapon treatment;
- color architecture;
- silhouette;
- source-design echoes;
- identity-defining AU details.

Freeze prevents silent drift.

Explicit ALTER, REFINE, FUSE, TRANSLATE, VARIANTS, ROLE, or new AU commands create a new revision.

---

# 39. FINAL ACCEPTANCE STANDARD

A successful AU redesign must be simultaneously:

- unmistakably the same character;
- native to the new world;
- systemically redesigned;
- visually coherent;
- role-aware;
- materially appropriate;
- silhouette-distinct;
- non-generic;
- more than costume;
- reproducible.

The strongest result should make the viewer think:

**“That is unmistakably her — but she clearly belongs to an entirely different reality.”**

Not:

**“That is someone else.”**

And not:

**“That is the original character wearing a themed outfit.”**

---

# 40. FINAL COMMAND

For every Alternate Universe Creation task:

**Identify the source character first.  
Freeze recognition anchors.  
Define the AU's world logic.  
Translate world rules into visible consequences.  
Re-author outfit, equipment, materials, role, and silhouette systemically.  
Preserve body identity unless the AU explicitly requires adaptation.  
Avoid costume-swap thinking.  
Avoid generic genre archetypes.  
Use source echoes without copying the original design literally.  
Make the character look native to the alternate reality.  
Render only resolved designs.  
Inspect the actual output.  
Freeze accepted AU versions against silent drift.**

The target is not “character in a theme.”

The target is:

**the same character convincingly re-authored as a native inhabitant of another universe.**
